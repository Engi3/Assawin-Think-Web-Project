Accuracy vs Precision

Accuracy = how close the cluster's center is to the bullseye. Precision = how tight the cluster is, regardless of where it's centered. A device can be precise but inaccurate (tight, off-center) or accurate but imprecise (centered, scattered).

Knowledge Notes

  • Accuracy class on a datasheet is usually stated as ± % of full scale (FS), not ± % of the actual reading — a 0.5% FS error on a 0-100 bar transducer is always ±0.5 bar, even at a reading of 5 bar.
  • Repeatability vs. reproducibility: repeatability is the same operator/equipment/conditions repeated; reproducibility allows different operators, equipment, or labs.
  • Industrial sensors are typically re-calibrated on a 6-12 month cycle, traceable to a reference standard per ISO/IEC 17025-accredited labs.
  • 4-20mA current loops are preferred over 0-10V for long cable runs because current doesn't degrade from cable resistance/noise the way voltage does — 0mA also unambiguously means "broken wire" (0V could just mean "zero value").
  • Resolution is not accuracy: a display showing 4 decimal places can still be wildly wrong if the sensor itself is poorly calibrated.

Measurement Chain & a First Ladder Rung

|--[ X0 ]--------------------------------( Y0 )--|  // X0: guard-door limit switch    Y0: indicator lamp
Applied example: a maintenance technician wires a simple digital sensor (a limit switch on a machine guard door) straight into PLC input X0. As soon as the door opens, X0 turns ON and the ladder rung energizes output Y0, lighting a warning lamp. Every sensor in Units 2-6 plugs into this same input → PLC → output pattern — only the sensor technology and the math in between get more advanced.
