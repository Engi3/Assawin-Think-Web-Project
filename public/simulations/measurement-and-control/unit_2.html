Speed & Position Sensors

Encoders turn rotation into pulses; proximity sensors detect a target without touching it. Both feed a PLC's digital input or High-Speed Counter module — sweep the controls below to see how.

Incremental mode: count A/B pulses over time, divide by PPR, to get speed — the phase of A vs B gives direction. Speed = (pulses/sec ÷ PPR) × 60.

The sensor switches ON once the target is closer than its rated sensing distance Sn. Inductive sensors only trigger on metal targets; capacitive sensors trigger on almost any material (including liquids and plastics).

Knowledge Notes

  • NPN vs. PNP is a regional convention as much as a technical one — many older/legacy Asian control panels standardize on NPN (sinking), European/newer panels favor PNP (sourcing). Always match the sensor to the PLC input card's wiring type before installing.
  • Higher PPR = finer resolution but a steeper frequency demand: a 1000 PPR encoder at 3000 RPM outputs 50,000 pulses/sec — check your High-Speed Counter module can actually count that fast.
  • Absolute encoders cost more but eliminate the "homing" routine most incremental setups need after every power loss — worth it on safety-critical or hard-to-access axes.
  • Inductive sensor sensing distance is derated for non-ferrous metals — a sensor rated for 8mm on steel may only reliably detect aluminum at ~3-4mm.
  • Mechanical limit switches need debounce handling in the PLC program (a short timer) to avoid false multiple triggers from contact bounce.

NPN/PNP Wiring & a Position-Stop Ladder

|--[ HSC1 >= K1000 ]-----------------------------( Y0 )--|  // HSC1: encoder pulse count   Y0: stop conveyor motor
Applied example: a High-Speed Counter (HSC) instruction accumulates encoder pulses from a conveyor's drive roller. When the count reaches a preset (K1000 = the pulse count for one exact travel distance), the rung de-energizes the drive motor output Y0 — indexing the conveyor to a precise stop position every cycle, the same principle used to home a robotic arm axis.
