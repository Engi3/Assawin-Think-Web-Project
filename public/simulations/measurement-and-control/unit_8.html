      Knowledge Notes

      • MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) measures reliability between breakdowns; MTTR (Mean Time To Repair) measures how fast a failure gets fixed — both feed into overall equipment availability calculations.
      • 5S (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain) is a workplace-organization discipline that directly supports maintenance: a clean, organized panel makes wiring faults and loose connections visible at a glance.
      • A strong safety culture treats near-miss reports (an incident that almost happened) as seriously as actual accidents — near-misses are free lessons that prevent the real thing.
      • Safety-critical circuits (like emergency stops) commonly use dual-channel monitoring with a certified safety relay — two independent contacts must both open, so a single wire or contact failure can't silently disable the E-stop.
      • Predictive maintenance (vibration analysis, thermal imaging, oil analysis) goes a step beyond scheduled preventive maintenance — it catches developing faults based on real condition data, not just a fixed calendar interval.

      A Dual-Channel E-Stop Safety Interlock

      |--[ X10 ]--[ X11 ]---------------------------------( Y10 )--|  // X10/X11: dual-channel E-stop (NC) -> safety relay OK
|--[ Y10 ]------------------------------------------( Y0 )--|  // Y10 permits main motor/actuator output Y0
      Applied example: both channels of a dual-contact emergency stop button (X10, X11) must be closed (button not pressed) for the safety-OK bit Y10 to energize; only then can the main drive output Y0 ever turn on. Before opening the panel to check any sensor from Units 1-7, this is exactly the circuit a technician verifies is de-energized as part of the Lockout–Tagout walkthrough above.
