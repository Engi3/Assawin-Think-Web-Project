Pressure & Flow Sensors

Pressure transducers and switches protect and monitor pneumatic / hydraulic systems; flow meters track how much fluid is moving through a pipe. Handle real pressurized systems with care.

Knowledge Notes

  • Pressure switches have a differential (hysteresis) setting, not just one trip point — without a gap between the "trip" and "reset" pressure, the switch would chatter rapidly right at the setpoint.
  • Gauge vs. absolute vs. vacuum pressure: gauge pressure is relative to atmosphere (most common industrially), absolute pressure is relative to a perfect vacuum, and vacuum gauges read negative gauge pressure below atmosphere.
  • Flow meters need a straight, undisturbed pipe run before and after the sensor (often 10x pipe diameter upstream, 5x downstream) — elbows and valves too close by cause turbulence that skews the reading.
  • Electromagnetic flow meters require a conductive fluid (like water with dissolved minerals) to work at all — they cannot measure oil, pure deionized water, or air/gas flow.
  • Sudden valve closures in liquid-filled pipes cause water hammer — pressure transducers near pumps/valves should be rated well above normal operating pressure to survive these transient spikes.

2-Wire Loop Wiring & a Pump Protection Ladder

|--[ X1 ]--[ X2 ]-------------------------------( Y3 )--|  // X1: start PB  X2: low-pressure switch (NC)  Y3: pump run
Applied example: a pump start rung includes a normally-closed low-pressure switch (X2) in series with the start pushbutton (X1). If suction-side pressure drops too low (a sign the pump is about to run dry and self-destruct), X2 opens and immediately drops output Y3, stopping the pump — the same pattern protects compressed air systems from running a compressor against a closed valve.
