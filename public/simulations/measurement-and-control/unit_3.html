Knowledge Notes

  • Accuracy class (OIML R60: C2, C3, C6...) governs legal-for-trade weighing — a higher class number means more verification intervals, i.e. finer usable resolution for the same capacity.
  • 4-wire vs. 6-wire load cells: 6-wire adds two "sense" wires that measure the actual excitation voltage at the cell (not just what the amplifier sends), compensating for voltage drop in long/temperature-affected cables.
  • Load cell body shapes are chosen by application: shear beam (platform scales), single-point (small retail/bench scales), canister/compression (tank/hopper weighing, high capacity).
  • Torque transducers on a rotating shaft need a slip ring or wireless telemetry to get the strain-gauge signal off the spinning shaft; reaction torque transducers avoid this by measuring a stationary reaction arm instead.
  • Always check the safe overload rating (often 150% of rated capacity) — momentary overloads from impact loading are a leading cause of load cell failure.

6-Wire Load Cell Wiring & an Overload-Alarm Ladder

|--[ D0 >= K9500 ]------------------------------( Y1 )--|  // D0: scaled weight in kg x10   Y1: overload alarm
Applied example: a batching hopper's scaled weight (D0, already converted from mV to kg via Unit 7's scaling method) is compared against 95.0 kg (K9500 with one decimal place implied). Crossing that value energizes an overload alarm output Y1 before the hopper reaches its rated capacity — the same comparison-rung pattern protects conveyor scales and monitors motor torque limits.
